Can You Get a Mortgage With a Seasonal Job? A Grand Strand Guide

Work the season in Myrtle Beach? Here's how lenders count your income, including winter unemployment.

Paul Hankins
Founder & Broker · NMLS #281393

Yes, you can get a mortgage with a seasonal job. On the Grand Strand, plenty of people earn most of their money between spring and fall, at restaurants, golf courses, rental companies, attractions and beach services. Lenders can count that income. What they need is a two-year track record, so they can see it comes back every season.

What lenders need to see

For a conventional loan, Fannie Mae's rule for seasonal income asks for three things:

  1. Two years of history. You've done seasonal work for at least the last two years.
  2. Proof of the income. Your most recent pay stub and your last two years of W-2s, or a written verification from your employer. The lender also calls your employer to confirm.
  3. An average. The lender adds your income so far this year to the last two years, and averages it. That average is your qualifying income.

Because it's an average, a slow year and a busy year can even each other out. But if your income has been dropping, a lender may use the lower, more recent figure instead.

Does winter unemployment count as income?

It can. With a conventional loan, if you collect unemployment every off-season, a lender can count it as income when:

  • it clearly comes from your seasonal layoff;
  • it's expected to happen again next year;
  • it shows on your last two years of signed federal tax returns.

Keep your tax returns, because they're how the lender sees the full pattern of your year.

An example. Jake works at a golf course in North Myrtle Beach. His year looks like this:

MonthsWhat he earns
March to NovemberWages from the golf course
December to FebruarySeasonal unemployment, until the course hires him back

He's done this for three years, and it all shows on his signed tax returns. His lender averages his wages over this year and the two before, adds his seasonal unemployment, and counts the total as his monthly income.

Own a seasonal business?

If you run your own business, like a beach rental company, a food truck or a charter boat, your tax returns may not show what you really earn after write-offs. A bank statement loan looks at 12 to 24 months of deposits instead, which captures both the busy months and the slow ones. Its terms differ from a regular loan, so compare it with a conventional loan if your tax returns can support one. See also how many bank statements you need for a mortgage.

Less than two years of seasonal work?

A conventional loan needs the two-year history. If you're newer to seasonal work, talk to a loan officer anyway. BrickWood is a broker, so we can check how different lenders treat your history.

Get your paperwork ready

  • Your last two years of W-2s and federal tax returns
  • Your most recent pay stub
  • Records of any seasonal unemployment you received
  • If you're self-employed, 12 to 24 months of bank statements

Seasonal income questions

Can you get a mortgage with a seasonal job?

Yes. Lenders can count seasonal income when you have at least a two-year history of it. They average your income from this year and the previous two years.

Does unemployment count as income for a mortgage?

With a conventional loan, seasonal unemployment can count when it clearly comes from a seasonal layoff, is expected to recur, and shows on your last two years of signed federal tax returns.

How do lenders calculate seasonal income?

Under Fannie Mae's rule, the lender averages your year-to-date income with the previous two years of earnings, using your pay stub and W-2s or a verification from your employer.

I own a seasonal business. What loan works?

A bank statement loan can use 12 to 24 months of business or personal deposits instead of tax returns, which captures both busy and slow months.
