The short answer

A beach house is a second home loan when you use it yourself for part of the year, keep control of it, and do not need its rent to qualify. You can still rent it out at times. It is an investment property loan when you will not use it yourself, when a rental company controls who stays there, or when you need the rent to qualify.

Which one is your beach house?

Your lender sorts every home loan by how you will use the home: the home you live in, a second home, or an investment property. For a second home, Fannie Mae requires all of these:

  • you live in it for some part of the year;
  • it is a single home or a single condo unit, not a duplex or larger;
  • it can be lived in all year;
  • you have exclusive control of it;
  • it is not a timeshare, and no agreement gives a management company control over who stays there.

Can you rent out a second home?

Yes. Fannie Mae allows a second home loan even when the lender sees rental income from the property. Two conditions apply: the rent is not used to qualify you for the loan, and you meet every other second home rule, including living there part of the year.

The line is control. You may list the house yourself or hire a manager you can fire. You may not sign an agreement that hands control over who stays there to a management company, such as a condo rental pool with blackout dates for owners.

If your income is not enough without the rent, the loan is an investment property loan. For an investment purchase, a DSCR loan can qualify you on the rent the house brings in instead of your personal income.

What the label changes

The label changes three things about your loan.

  1. The down payment. Fannie Mae sets a higher minimum down payment for an investment property than for a second home, and a higher one for a second home than for the home you live in.
  2. The price of the loan. Fannie Mae adds a pricing charge to every investment property loan and to some second home loans. It shows up as a higher rate or as points.
  3. What you must show. Credit score minimums and the cash you must keep in reserve differ by occupancy. Second home and investment loans must both be approved through Fannie Mae's automated underwriting system.

The South Carolina property tax difference

South Carolina taxes a second home more than the home you live in. The county assesses your legal residence at 4% of its market value, if you apply for that rate. A second home or a rental is assessed at 6%.

When we pre-approve you, we use the 6% tax figure for a second home, so the approval matches the bill you will get.

Why the label must be true

You sign a statement at closing saying how you will use the home. Calling a rental a second home to get better terms is a false statement on a loan application, which is a federal crime. If your plans are not settled, tell us. We will set up the loan for how you actually plan to use the house.

Plans can change later. If you buy a second home and years later turn it into a full-time rental, that is a different matter from misstating your plans at closing. Ask us before you change how you use a home with a loan on it.
