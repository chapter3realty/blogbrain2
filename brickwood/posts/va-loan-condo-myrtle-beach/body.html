Yes, you can use a VA loan to buy a condo in Myrtle Beach, but the building has to be approved by VA first. VA reviews each condo project's legal documents before it will back a loan on any unit. You can check a building's status in minutes, and if it isn't approved, you still have options.

How to check if a condo is VA-approved

  1. Go to VA's Condo Report search.
  2. Search by the condo's name, city or county. Try a short version of the name, because projects are often listed under their legal association name, not the name on the sign.
  3. Look at the status. The list also includes projects VA rejected, suspended or hasn't finished reviewing, so don't stop at finding the name. You can tick "Approved" to see only approved projects. If it's approved, you can use your VA benefit there.

A recent search for Horry County showed 229 condo projects, including 28 that VA rejected and 11 still pending. Anyone can run the search, so check it before you fall for a unit. When you pre-qualify, tell your loan officer the building name too.

What VA looks at

VA's approval is about the condo's legal setup. VA reviews the documents that create the condo, such as the declaration and bylaws, to make sure they meet its rules, for example on how owners can sell their units. This review is separate from the VA appraisal, which every VA loan still needs on the unit itself.

VA's list is also separate from FHA's and Fannie Mae's. A building can be approved for one and not the others. If you're comparing loans, check each one. Our guide to Myrtle Beach condo financing explains the Fannie Mae and FHA side.

What if the building isn't VA-approved?

You have a few paths:

  • Ask for approval. The condo's documents can be sent to VA for review. It takes time, and the association has to cooperate, so start as early as you can.
  • Use a different loan. An FHA loan works if the building is FHA-approved, or the unit can get FHA approval on its own. A conventional loan works if the building passes Fannie Mae's review.
  • Use a non-warrantable condo loan. If the building doesn't qualify for the big programs, some of our lenders make condo loans outside those rules.

The trade-off: other loans don't come with VA's benefits, like no monthly mortgage insurance. Compare them side by side before you give up on the VA loan.

How much can you borrow with a VA loan on a condo?

If you have your full VA entitlement, VA sets no loan limit. You can borrow what a lender approves you for, as long as the appraisal supports the price. If part of your entitlement is tied up in another VA loan, Horry County's conforming limit is used to figure how much of your new loan VA will guarantee. Details are in our guide to Horry County loan limits.

Don't forget the HOA dues and insurance

Your monthly HOA dues count in the budget a lender reviews, along with your mortgage, taxes and insurance. If the building is in a high-risk flood zone, flood insurance is part of the cost, usually through the HOA's master policy, and you may need a policy of your own too. An example. A Navy veteran moving to the Grand Strand finds a two-bedroom condo in North Myrtle Beach. Before making an offer, she searches VA's Condo Report and finds the building approved. She tells her loan officer the building name with her pre-qualification, and the VA loan goes ahead on the unit's appraisal. Her friend's building down the street wasn't on the list, so he compared an FHA loan instead.
