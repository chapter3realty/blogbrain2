Yes, you can get a mortgage with a seasonal job. On the Grand Strand, plenty of people earn most of their money between spring and fall, at restaurants, golf courses, rental companies, attractions and beach services. Lenders can count that income. What they need is a two-year track record, so they can see it comes back every season.

What lenders need to see

For a conventional loan, Fannie Mae's rule for seasonal income asks for three things:

  1. Two years of history. You've done seasonal work for at least the last two years.
  2. Proof of the income. Your most recent pay stub and your last two years of W-2s, or a written verification from your employer. The lender also calls your employer to confirm.
  3. An average. The lender adds your income so far this year to the last two years, and averages it. That average is your qualifying income.

Because it's an average, a slow year and a busy year even each other out. A lender isn't looking only at what you made last summer.

Does winter unemployment count as income?

It can. If you collect unemployment every off-season, a lender can count it as income when:

  • it clearly comes from your seasonal layoff;
  • it's expected to happen again next year;
  • you've received it for at least two years;
  • it shows on your personal tax returns.

That last point matters. Keep your tax returns, because they're how the lender sees the full pattern of your year.

An example. Jake works at a golf course in North Myrtle Beach from March through November. Each winter he's laid off and collects unemployment until the course hires him back. He's done this for three years, and it all shows on his tax returns. His lender averages his wages over the past two years plus this year, adds his seasonal unemployment, and counts the total as his income.

Timing your application

Lenders average your year-to-date income with the two years before it. In spring, before your season gets going, your year-to-date number is small. Ask your loan officer how the timing affects your numbers before you apply.

Own a seasonal business?

If you run your own business, like a beach rental company, a food truck or a charter boat, your tax returns may not show what you really earn after write-offs. A bank statement loan looks at 12 to 24 months of deposits instead, which captures both the busy months and the slow ones. See also how many bank statements you need for a mortgage.

Less than two years of seasonal work?

A conventional loan needs the two-year history. If you're newer to seasonal work, talk to a loan officer anyway. BrickWood is a broker, so we can check how different lenders treat your history.

Get your paperwork ready

  • Your last two years of W-2s and federal tax returns
  • Your most recent pay stub
  • Records of any seasonal unemployment you received
  • If you're self-employed, 12 to 24 months of bank statements
