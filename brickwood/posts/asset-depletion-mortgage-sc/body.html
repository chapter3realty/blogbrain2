An asset depletion loan lets you qualify for a mortgage with your savings instead of a paycheck. The lender adds up your accounts, sets aside what you need to buy the home, and turns the rest into a monthly income figure. That figure counts just like a salary.

It is built for people with money in the bank but little income on paper:

  • retirees living on savings;
  • people who sold a business or a property;
  • people between jobs or taking time off;
  • investors who live off their accounts.

How asset depletion works

Lenders use a simple formula:

(Your eligible assets − down payment − closing costs − reserves) ÷ a set number of months = your monthly qualifying income

Reserves are the savings a lender wants you to still have after closing. Whatever is left after the purchase gets spread over the months, and that monthly amount is your income for the loan.

The number of months makes a big difference. Fannie Mae's version divides by the months in the loan: 360 for a 30-year mortgage. Some of the lenders we work with divide by fewer months. One divides by just 60. That 60 is only used to figure your income.

An example. Linda is 66. She sold her business in New Jersey and wants a house in Pawleys Island. She has $1.4 million in savings and investments, but no paycheck. After her down payment, closing costs and reserves, $900,000 of eligible assets are left. A lender that divides by 360 months counts $2,500 a month as her income. A lender that divides by 60 months counts $15,000 a month. Same savings, very different buying power. That is why the lender you choose matters.

What counts as an asset

Lenders don't count every dollar the same way. Here is how one of our lenders counts them:

Type of accountHow much counts
Checking, savings, money market100%
Money from selling real estate you owned100%
Stocks, bonds, mutual funds70%
Retirement accounts, if you are 59½ or older70%
Retirement accounts, if you are under 59½60%

Fannie Mae's version is stricter. It counts only retirement accounts and a retirement or severance payout from an employer. Money from selling a business or a house does not count under Fannie Mae's rules, but it does with lenders like the one above.

Asset depletion loan requirements

Every lender sets its own rules. Here are the rules at one of the lenders we work with:

  • Enough assets. At least $1 million, or 150% of the loan amount, whichever is lower. This minimum doesn't apply if you are using your assets to add to other income, like Social Security or a pension.
  • Seasoned money. The money has been in a U.S. account for at least 120 days. Money from selling real estate you owned doesn't need to wait.
  • Recent statements. Your 4 most recent monthly statements, recent quarterly statements, or a verification form from your bank.
  • Money you can reach. The accounts must be available to you without a penalty.
  • Everyone on the loan. Anyone named on the accounts must also sign the loan.
  • The lower credit score. If two of you apply, the lender uses the lower of your scores.

Moving to the Grand Strand after selling your home?

If you're selling a house in another state first, this helps. With the lender above, money from selling a home you owned counts in full and doesn't have to sit in your account for 120 days first. That makes the timing much easier when you sell up north and buy at the beach.

South Carolina also doesn't tax Social Security, and at 65 or older, after a year as a resident, the first $50,000 of your home's value is exempt from property taxes. Both help the monthly budget once you've moved.

Can you combine it with Social Security or a pension?

Yes. Your asset income is added to your Social Security, pension or other income, and the lender looks at the total. With that lender, the $1 million minimum is waived when your assets are only adding to other income.

Do you have to sell your investments?

No. The lender only needs to see that the money is yours and that you can reach it. Your accounts can stay invested. The formula is a way to measure what you can afford, not a plan to drain your savings.

Asset depletion or a bank statement loan?

If you are self-employed and your business deposits are steady, a bank statement loan may fit better. It looks at the money flowing through your accounts each month. Asset depletion looks at the money sitting in them. If you are buying a rental, a DSCR loan qualifies you on the rent instead.
