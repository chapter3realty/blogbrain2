When a lender requires flood insurance

Flood insurance is required on your loan when the house sits in a Special Flood Hazard Area, FEMA's name for a high-risk flood zone. Every zone that starts with the letter A or V is one. Along the Grand Strand you will see AE zones near marshes and rivers and VE zones on the oceanfront, where waves add to the risk.

We order the flood zone check, a FEMA Standard Flood Hazard Determination, when you apply. If the house is in a flood zone, you get a written notice before closing. A house is in the zone if any part of the main house is in it. A shed or detached garage that is not a home does not need flood insurance when the house itself is outside the zone.

The rule covers conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae, loans from federally regulated banks, and FHA and VA loans. It also applies in a protected coastal barrier area, land that Congress set aside along the coast, even outside an A or V zone.

One more rule matters in a few places. If the town or county does not take part in the National Flood Insurance Program and the house is in a flood zone, Fannie Mae will not buy the loan. Horry County and the beach towns we lend in take part, but we check every address.

How much flood coverage the lender requires

The federal minimum is the lower of your loan balance or the most the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) offers for that kind of building. Fannie Mae adds a third measure. You need the lowest of these three:

MeasureFor a typical house
The cost to rebuild the houseFrom the insurance company's estimate
The NFIP maximum$250,000 for a single-family building
Your loan amountFrom your loan

The required coverage is on the building. Flood insurance never covers the land. Coverage for your belongings is a separate policy choice.

How the flood premium affects your approval

The yearly flood premium becomes part of your monthly housing cost, the same as property tax and homeowners insurance. Lenders compare that monthly total with your income. A high premium can lower the price you qualify for.

Federally regulated lenders must collect the flood premium in your escrow account with each monthly payment, with a few exceptions. The first year's premium is usually paid at closing.

There is no waiting period when you buy flood insurance for a new loan. An NFIP policy bought with a mortgage takes effect at closing, as long as the application and premium are in by then. Without a loan, a new NFIP policy normally waits 30 days.

Before you offer, send us the address. We order the flood zone check, and once you have an insurance quote, we show you how the premium changes the price you qualify for. Premiums vary from house to house, based on each home's own flood risk, how high it sits and what it would cost to rebuild. That is why two houses on the same street can have very different bills. Getting a house out of the flood zone

Under Fannie Mae's rule, flood insurance is not required once the lender has a letter from FEMA saying its map has been amended and the house is no longer in a Special Flood Hazard Area. This is a Letter of Map Amendment, or LOMA. Owners usually apply with an elevation certificate showing the house sits above the flood level. If the house you want already has a LOMA, we get a copy through the seller's agent and include it with your loan.

A house outside the zone can still flood. You may buy flood insurance even when the lender does not require it. Our affiliate explains how to check whether a house has flooded before.

NFIP policy or a private flood policy

You can use either. Federal rules require regulated lenders to accept a private flood policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance. Fannie Mae accepts a private policy whose coverage and terms are at least equal to an NFIP policy, from an insurer with an acceptable rating. Send us the full policy, not only a quote, and we confirm it meets the rules before closing.

Condos and the HOA's master flood policy

For an attached condo in a flood zone, the HOA must carry a master flood policy on your unit's building. Fannie Mae requires coverage of at least the lower of 80% of the cost to rebuild the building or the NFIP maximum per unit. If the master policy's share for your unit falls short of what your loan needs, you buy a separate policy for the difference. We read the HOA's policy as part of the building review.
