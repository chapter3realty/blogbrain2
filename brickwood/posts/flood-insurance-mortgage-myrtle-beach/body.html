Flood insurance is required for a mortgage when the home is in a high-risk flood zone, which is any FEMA flood zone that starts with A or V. The rule covers almost every home loan: conventional, FHA, VA and bank loans. It is also required in a few protected coastal barrier areas. Outside those areas, flood insurance is usually your choice.

How do you know if a house is in a flood zone?

Anyone can look up an address on FEMA's flood map. Once you apply for a loan, the lender orders an official flood zone check on the home. If it is in a high-risk zone, you'll get a written notice before closing, and flood insurance becomes part of the loan.

Here is what the letters on the map mean:

ZoneWhat it meansRequired for your loan?
Starts with A (like AE)High risk.Yes
Starts with V (like VE)High risk on the oceanfront, where waves add to the flooding.Yes
XModerate or low risk.No, but you can still buy it

A shed, or a detached garage with no living space, in the zone doesn't trigger the rule when the house itself is outside it.

Is flood insurance required in Zone X?

Usually not. Your lender won't require it in Zone X. But Zone X isn't "no risk", and homes there do flood. Most homeowners insurance doesn't cover flooding, so you need a separate flood policy.

How much flood insurance does the lender require?

The lender needs coverage on the building equal to the lowest of these three amounts:

  • what it would cost to rebuild the house;
  • $250,000, the most a standard federal flood policy covers on a house;
  • your loan amount.

Coverage for your furniture and belongings is usually your choice. The required coverage is on the house, not the land under it.

How flood insurance affects what you can afford

The flood premium becomes part of your monthly housing cost, just like property taxes and homeowners insurance. Lenders compare that total with your income. So a high flood premium can lower the price you qualify for.

An example. Two houses have the same price. One is in Zone X. The other sits in an AE zone near the marsh. The AE house needs a flood policy, and that premium is added to the monthly cost. With the same income, the buyer qualifies for a little less on the AE house.

A few things to know:

  • Get a quote early. Premiums are priced home by home, based on things like how high the house sits, how close it is to water and what it would cost to rebuild. Two houses on the same street can have very different bills.
  • It's usually paid through escrow. Regulated lenders generally collect the premium with your monthly payment.
  • No waiting period when you buy. A federal flood policy bought with your new loan starts at closing, as long as you apply and pay by closing. Without a loan, a new policy normally waits 30 days.

Can you use private flood insurance?

Yes. Lenders accept private flood policies that give at least the same coverage as a federal policy. Your insurance agent can quote both.

Can a house be taken out of a flood zone?

Sometimes. If the ground right next to the house is higher than the flood level on the map, the owner can ask FEMA for a Letter of Map Amendment, called a LOMA. They send FEMA elevation data, such as a surveyor's certificate. If FEMA agrees, the lender no longer requires flood insurance on that house. If the home you're buying already has a LOMA, ask the seller for a copy.

Buying a condo in a flood zone?

In a condo building in a high-risk zone, the lender looks for a master flood policy the HOA carries on the building. If the HOA's coverage for your unit falls short of what your loan needs, you buy a policy of your own to fill the gap. For more on condo loans, see condo loan requirements.
