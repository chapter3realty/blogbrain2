If you're buying in Myrtle Beach or anywhere on the Grand Strand, your lender will require flood insurance when the home sits in a high-risk flood zone. That's any FEMA zone starting with A or V. The rule applies to most home loans, including conventional, FHA and VA loans and loans from federally regulated banks. It also applies in a few protected coastal barrier areas. Outside those areas, flood insurance is usually your choice.

Myrtle Beach flood zone map: how to look up a house

Type the address into FEMA's flood map. Horry County also posts its FEMA flood maps on the county site. The maps in effect for Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach, Conway and the rest of Horry County date from December 16, 2021. In Georgetown County, the Town of Pawleys Island's map dates from May 9, 2023. The map for the rest of the county, outside the City of Georgetown, dates from June 18, 2025.

Every home on the map is in some flood zone. The question is whether your zone makes flood insurance a requirement for your loan. Here is what the letters mean:

ZoneWhat it meansRequired for your loan?
Starts with A (like AE)High risk.Yes
Starts with V (like VE)High risk on the oceanfront, where waves add to the flooding.Yes
Shaded X (called B on older maps)Moderate risk. Usually between the 100-year and 500-year flood lines, or shallow flooding.No, but you can still buy it
Unshaded X (called C on older maps)Low risk. Usually above the 500-year flood line.No, but you can still buy it

Once you're under contract and apply, the lender orders a flood certificate (often called a flood cert) on the home. It's the official record of the home's flood zone. If the home is in a high-risk zone, you'll get a written notice before closing. A shed, or a detached garage with no living space, in the zone doesn't trigger the rule when the house itself is outside it.

Is flood insurance required in Zone X?

Usually not. Your lender won't require it in shaded or unshaded Zone X. But Zone X isn't "no risk", and homes there can flood, often from heavy rain and drainage problems. FEMA recommends flood insurance there. Most homeowners insurance doesn't cover flooding, so you need a separate flood policy to be covered. In the City of Myrtle Beach, a federal flood policy on a Zone X home also gets the 10% discount above.

How much flood insurance does the lender require?

The lender needs coverage on the building equal to the lowest of these three amounts:

  • what it would cost to rebuild the house;
  • $250,000, the most a standard federal flood policy covers on a house;
  • your loan amount.

Coverage for your furniture and belongings is usually your choice. The required coverage is on the house, not the land under it.

How much is flood insurance in Myrtle Beach?

There's no one number. Federal flood policies are priced home by home, based on how high the house sits, how close it is to water and what it would cost to rebuild. Two houses on the same street can have very different bills. Get a quote on the house you want before you make an offer.

The premium then becomes part of your monthly housing cost, just like taxes and homeowners insurance. Lenders compare that total with your income, so a high flood premium can lower the price you qualify for.

An example. Two Horry County houses have the same price. One is in Zone X. The other sits in an AE zone near the marsh. The AE house needs a flood policy, and that premium is added to the monthly cost. With the same income, the buyer qualifies for a little less on the AE house.

A few more things to know:

  • It's usually paid through escrow. Regulated lenders generally collect the premium with your monthly payment.
  • No waiting period when you buy. A federal flood policy bought with your new loan starts at closing, as long as you apply and pay by closing. Without a loan, a new policy normally waits 30 days.
  • Private flood insurance works too. Lenders accept private policies that give at least the same coverage as a federal policy.

Can an elevation certificate lower your flood insurance?

It can. An elevation certificate is a form a surveyor fills out showing how high the house sits compared with the flood level on the map. FEMA no longer requires one to buy a federal flood policy. It estimates each home's height from its own data. But if the house sits higher than FEMA's estimate, giving the certificate to your insurance agent may lower your premium.

Ask the seller early whether they have one. If not, your agent can tell you whether paying a surveyor for one is likely to help on that house.

Can a house be taken out of a flood zone?

Sometimes. If the ground right next to the house is higher than the flood level on the map, the owner can ask FEMA for a Letter of Map Amendment, called a LOMA. If FEMA agrees, the lender no longer requires flood insurance on that house. If the home you're buying already has one, ask the seller for a copy.

Buying a condo in a flood zone?

For an oceanfront or marsh-side condo in a high-risk zone, the lender looks for a master flood policy the HOA carries on the building.
