Moving to Myrtle Beach but still own your home up north? You don't have to sell first. A buy-before-you-sell program, a bridge loan or a HELOC can turn the equity in your current home into the down payment on your new one. Then you move once, and sell on your own schedule.

The hard part of buying before you sell is usually one of three things: you need the cash from your old home for the down payment, a lender won't approve you while you still carry the old mortgage, or a seller won't accept an offer that depends on your home selling. Each option below solves a different piece of that.

1. A buy-before-you-sell program

This is the most complete option, and it's one we offer through one of our lenders. Here's how it works:

  1. Get your current home approved. The program reviews it. Approval usually comes within 24 to 48 hours.
  2. Make a stronger offer. The program places a backup offer on your current home. That means you can offer on your Grand Strand home with no "my house has to sell first" clause, and your old mortgage no longer counts against what you can afford.
  3. Close on the new home. If you need your equity for the down payment, the program can unlock a large part of it and send it to closing. How much depends on your home, what you still owe and your finances.
  4. Sell your old home on your timeline, with the agent of your choice, at the price you and your agent set.

If your old home hasn't sold within 120 days after you buy, the program buys it at its backup offer, then sells it on the open market. You receive the net profits from that sale, less the program fee and the costs of holding the home. The fee is paid when your old home sells, not when you buy.

Already have the cash for your down payment? There's a lighter version that only removes your old mortgage from the math and the sale contingency from your offer.

The program covers homes from 750 to 5,500 square feet on up to five acres, with up to $2 million in equity.

2. A second-lien bridge loan

A bridge loan is a short-term loan against your old home. It sits behind your current mortgage as a second lien, and it gives you the cash for your down payment. When your old home sells, the sale pays it off. BrickWood arranges these through its lenders too.

The thing to plan for: until the old home sells, you own two homes. A loan officer will check that you can qualify with both on your budget, and that the timing works.

3. A HELOC on your current home

A home equity line of credit lets you borrow against the home you're leaving, as you need it. Set it up before you list, and use it for the down payment on the beach house. You pay it off when the old home sells.

HELOCs differ a lot from lender to lender, which is why we shop them. When we compare HELOCs for the same home, the results can be far apart:

  • The most one lender will lend can be close to double what another will, because each sets its own limit on how much of the home's value you can borrow against.
  • Some lenders let you choose between a lower upfront fee with a higher rate, or a higher fee with a lower rate.

How you repay a line also differs by lender, so compare the full terms, not just the amount. Your home secures a HELOC, so only borrow what you can repay; falling behind can put the home at risk. A HELOC is easier to set up before your home is listed for sale, so start early. Ask your loan officer about the timing.

4. An offer contingent on your sale

You can also make an offer that only goes through if your old house sells first. It costs nothing up front. But many sellers prefer an offer without that condition, especially on homes with a lot of interest. The buy-before-you-sell program exists largely to remove it.

An example. A couple from Pennsylvania finds a house in Murrells Inlet. Most of their savings are in their current home. Through the buy-before-you-sell program, they make a clean offer, close on the beach house with their equity as the down payment, then move once and list the old house the following month.
