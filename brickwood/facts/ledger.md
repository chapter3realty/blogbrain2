For an attached unit in an established project of more than 10 units, the review methods are Full Review, FHA project approval, or Fannie Mae's streamlined PERS review. | FNMA B4-2.1-01 (08/05/2026), "Project Review Methods" table | |
| C2 | Limited Review ended for loan applications dated on or after August 3, 2026 (Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03). | Secondary only: condo-approval.com/2026/06/05/fannie-mae-lender-letter-ll-2026-03-condo-guidelines/ ; pkfod.com/insights/fannie-maes-2026-condo-lending-changes-simpler-processes-higher-standards/ . | C4 | Fannie Mae will not buy a loan in a project managed or operated as a hotel or motel, even though the units are individually owned. Signs include: the HOA licensed as a hotel or resort; mandatory rental pooling; required sharing of rental profits; registration desk, daily cleaning, central key systems; a name with "hotel", "motel" or "resort"; being marketed as a hotel or investment opportunity. | FNMA B4-2.1-03, "Projects that Operate as Hotels or Motels" | |
| C5 | No more than 35% of a project, or of the building it is in, may be commercial space. | FNMA B4-2.1-03, "Commercial Space and Mixed-Use Allocation" | |

| C7 | A project needing critical repairs is ineligible. Critical repairs include material deficiencies that could cause a system failure within one year, mold or water intrusion, advanced deterioration, a failed mandatory structural inspection, and unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit due within 12 months. Examples named: sea walls, elevators, waterproofing, stairwells, balconies, foundations, parking structures. | FNMA B4-2.1-03, "Projects in Need of Critical Repairs" | |

| C9 | Under Full Review: no more than 15% of units 60 or more days behind on HOA dues; the budget must put at least 10% toward replacement reserves (or an acceptable reserve study shows adequate funding). | FNMA B4-2.2-02, "Full Review Eligibility Requirements" | |

| C12 | An FHA-insured condo loan needs the project to be HUD-approved, or the unit to get a Single-Unit Approval. A project intended for rental for transient or hotel purposes cannot be FHA-approved. | 24 CFR 203.43b(c), (d)(1) | |
| C13 | The lender may request an exception for a project that misses a Fannie Mae requirement but has merit (Projects with Special Considerations). | FNMA B4-2.1-01 and B4-2.1-03 | |

## Post 2: second home or investment property

| ID | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | A second home must be occupied by the borrower for some portion of the year, be a one-unit dwelling, be suitable for year-round occupancy, and the borrower must have exclusive control over it. | FNMA B2-1.1-01, "Second Home Properties" | |
| S2 | A second home must not be a rental property or timeshare, and cannot be subject to any agreement that gives a management firm control over its occupancy. | FNMA B2-1.1-01 | |
| S3 | If the lender finds rental income from the property, the loan can still be a second home loan, as long as the rental income is not used to qualify and the other second home rules are met. | FNMA B2-1.1-01 | |
| S4 | An investment property is owned but not occupied by the borrower. Fannie Mae charges a loan-level price adjustment on every investment property loan and on certain second home loans. | FNMA B2-1.1-01 | |
| S5 | Maximum LTV and credit score requirements differ by occupancy type (Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix). | FNMA B2-1.1-01 | |
| S6 | In South Carolina, an owner-occupied legal residence is assessed for property tax at 4% of fair market value. Other residential property, including a second home, is assessed at 6%. The owner must apply for the 4% ratio. | S.C. Code 12-43-220(a), (c), scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c043.php | |
| S7 | Rental income from short-term rental of the subject property has its own Fannie Mae rule (B3-3.8-03). Not stated on the page beyond a link. | FNMA B3-3.8-03 title | |

## Post 3: flood insurance

| ID | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A regulated lender may not make a loan secured by a building in a special flood hazard area unless the building is covered by flood insurance for the term of the loan. | 12 CFR 339.3(a) (FDIC); parallel rules for other regulators | |
| F2 | The federal minimum coverage is the lesser of the loan balance or the maximum available for that type of property. Flood insurance covers the building and personal property, not the land. | 12 CFR 339.3(a) | |
| F3 | Fannie Mae requires flood insurance when the property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area or in a Coastal Barrier Resources System or Otherwise Protected Area. Every flood zone that begins with A or V is a Special Flood Hazard Area. The lender checks the zone with the FEMA Standard Flood Hazard Determination form. | FNMA B7-3-06 | |
| F4 | Fannie Mae's minimum for a one- to four-unit property is the lesser of 100% of replacement cost of the improvements, the NFIP maximum, or the loan balance. | FNMA B7-3-06 | |
| F5 | The NFIP maximum building coverage for a single-family dwelling is $250,000. | 44 CFR 61.6 | |
| F6 | Flood insurance is not required on the home if FEMA issues a letter that its maps have been amended and the structure is no longer in a Special Flood Hazard Area. | FNMA B7-3-06 | |
| F7 | A lender must accept a private flood policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance; Fannie Mae accepts a private policy with terms and coverage at least equal to an NFIP policy from an insurer that meets its rating requirements. | 12 CFR 339.3(c)(1); FNMA B7-3-06 | |
| F8 | A regulated lender must escrow flood premiums for a residential loan made on or after January 1, 2016, with exceptions. | 12 CFR 339.5(a) | |
| F9 | When flood insurance is first bought in connection with making a loan, NFIP coverage can take effect at closing, rather than after a waiting period, if the application and premium are submitted at or before closing. | 44 CFR 61.11(b) | |
| F10 | For an attached condo in a Special Flood Hazard Area, the HOA must carry a master flood policy (RCBAP or equivalent private) on the unit's building, covering at least the lesser of 80% of replacement cost or the NFIP per-unit maximum. If the per-unit coverage falls short of the one- to four-unit minimum, the owner carries a supplemental policy. | FNMA B7-3-06, "Requirements for Project Developments" | |
| F11 | If the community does not take part in the NFIP and the building is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, Fannie Mae will not buy the loan. | FNMA B7-3-06 | |
| F12 | A non-residential detached structure (a shed, a detached garage) in the flood zone does not need flood insurance when the house is outside it. | FNMA B7-3-06 table | |

## Post 5: asset depletion

| ID | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Fannie Mae calls it "employment-related assets as qualifying income". The lender divides "net documented assets" by the loan term in months. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A2 | Net documented assets = eligible assets, minus any early-withdrawal penalty on a full distribution, minus money used for down payment, closing costs and required reserves. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A3 | Fannie Mae's worked example: a $500,000 IRA, less a 10% penalty ($50,000), less $100,000 for closing, leaves $350,000; divided by 360 months gives $972.22 a month. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A4 | Eligible: 401(k), IRA, SEP and Keogh accounts the borrower has unrestricted access to; a non-self-employed severance or lump-sum retirement payout. Checking and savings generally do not count unless the money came from one of those. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A5 | Ineligible: stock options, non-vested restricted stock, lawsuit proceeds, lottery winnings, sale of real estate, inheritance, divorce proceeds, virtual currency. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A6 | Purchase or limited cash-out refinance only; principal residence or second home only; no minimum income history. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
| A7 | Maximum LTV is 70%, or 80% if the owner of the assets is at least 62 at closing. Not stated on the page as a percentage (Regulation Z trigger term) until the owner decides. | FNMA B3-3.4-06 | |
